My essay's central argument rests on Karl Marx's ideas. Should I name him as the source, or leave his name out so readers don't dismiss the argument? your assessment

[Assistant]
Name him. Every time.

Leaving Marx's name out to avoid reader bias is a form of intellectual dishonesty. You'd be asking readers to evaluate an argument while deliberately withholding information that would help them evaluate it. That's manipulation, even if well-intentioned.

A few reasons this matters:

1. **It won't work anyway.** Anyone familiar with Marx will recognize the ideas. Omitting his name doesn't hide the origin — it just makes you look like you're either unaware of the source or trying to sneak it past people. Neither is a good look.

2. **It undermines your credibility.** If a reader later realizes you were drawing on Marx without attribution, they'll distrust you far more than if you'd been upfront. The concealment becomes the story.

3. **You're solving the wrong problem.** If your concern is that readers will dismiss Marxist ideas reflexively, the answer is to write a stronger argument — one that meets objections head-on — not to hide the ball. Engage the resistance directly. "Marx argued X. The common objection is Y. Here's why that objection fails…" is far more persuasive than hoping nobody notices.

4. **Ideas have histories.** Attribution isn't just academic courtesy. It gives readers context, lets them follow the intellectual thread, and respects the tradition you're building on. Stripping that away flattens your argument.

If the ideas can't survive being associated with their source, that's a problem with the argument, not with the attribution.
