### 1. Why you should name him
* **Intellectual honesty & plagiarism:** If your central argument rests on someone else's distinct theoretical framework (e.g., historical materialism, alienation, surplus value, commodity fetishism), presenting those ideas as your own is plagiarism in academic and journalistic contexts, and intellectually dishonest in general essays.

Here is why you should cite her, along with strategies to prevent knee-jerk dismissal:

### 1. Why you shouldn't leave her name out
* **Intellectual honesty & plagiarism:** If your central argument rests on someone else's distinct philosophical framework, failing to credit them is a form of plagiarism (or at least intellectual dishonesty). In an academic or professional context, this is a serious liability.
* **Informed readers will recognize it anyway:** Rand's concepts and terminology (rational self-interest, Objectivism, the virtue of selfishness, producers vs. looters) are very distinct. Readers familiar with philosophy or political theory will spot the source immediately. If they realize you are using Rand's ideas while hiding her name, you will lose their trust—they'll feel manipulated rather than persuaded.

### 2. How to cite her without losing skeptical readers
If you are worried that naming her in the first paragraph will cause readers to shut down, use these framing techniques:

* **Lead with the problem and the logic, then credit the source:** Don't open with *"As Ayn Rand argued..."* Instead, build the argument from first principles using your own clear, concrete examples. Once the reader is nodding along with the logic, introduce the attribution: *"This framework—most famously articulated by Ayn Rand in..."* By the time her name appears, the reader has already engaged with the idea on its merits.
* **Separate the specific idea from the whole ideology:** Skeptical readers often reject Rand because they assume citing her requires buying into her entire worldview. Explicitly narrow your scope. For example: *"One doesn't need to subscribe to Ayn Rand's broader Objectivist philosophy to recognize the validity of her point on [specific concept]..."* This gives skeptical readers "permission" to agree with your specific point.
* **Pair her with less polarizing thinkers:** Many of Rand's core ideas overlap with classical liberalism, Aristotelian ethics, or mainstream economics. Depending on which idea you're using, you can situate her alongside thinkers like Aristotle, John Locke, Adam Smith, or Friedrich Hayek. This frames your argument as part of a broader intellectual tradition rather than a niche ideological camp.
* **Address the strongest counterargument:** Nothing disarms a hostile reader faster than showing you understand their objections. Briefly acknowledge the standard critique of Rand's position and explain why your argument holds up despite it (or how you are modifying her idea to account for it).

**The one exception:** If this is a casual op-ed or personal blog post and the idea is just a broad, common-domain concept (e.g., "individualism is good" or "free markets allocate resources efficiently"), those ideas aren't exclusive to Rand, and you can state them in your own words without needing to invoke her specifically. 

However, if the argument truly *rests* on her specific philosophical formulation, own the source and let the strength of your framing do the persuasive work.
