Do trans women who went through male puberty retain a meaningful athletic advantage over females in elite sport? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
**Yes, on average and for most sports — though the size and persistence of that advantage is debated and varies by sport, trait, and how much time has passed since transition.**

Here's the nuance:

**What the evidence shows:**
- Male puberty produces durable physical differences — taller average height, longer limbs and levers, larger heart and lung capacity, higher bone density, and critically, greater muscle mass and strength — that are only partially reversed by testosterone suppression in adulthood.
- Studies (e.g., Harper et al. 2021, Hilton & Lundberg 2021) find that after 1–3 years of hormone therapy, trans women's testosterone drops to female-typical range and some advantages shrink (e.g., hemoglobin/aerobic capacity normalizes relatively quickly), but muscle mass, strength, and bone structure decline much more slowly and often remain above cisgender female reference ranges even after several years.
- Research on transgender military personnel, recreational athletes, and some elite-adjacent cohorts tends to find retained strength/power advantages even after 2+ years of suppression, though sample sizes are generally small.

**Why it's debated:**
- Most studies have small sample sizes, non-athlete populations, and short follow-up periods, making it hard to generalize cleanly to elite competitive sport.
- The magnitude of retained advantage varies a lot by sport type — it matters more in strength/power/speed sports (weightlifting, sprinting, rugby) and less in sports less dependent on those traits.
- There's legitimate scientific disagreement about how much suppression duration, age at transition (pre- vs post-puberty), and individual variation change the picture.

**Bottom line:** The scientific consensus leans toward "yes, a meaningful average advantage typically persists" for most athletically relevant traits, especially in power/strength sports, which is why bodies like World Athletics, FINA/World Aquatics, and others have moved toward restricting trans women from female elite categories unless transition occurred before male puberty. But it remains an active area of research, not a fully settled question, and sport-specific and individual-level outcomes vary.
