**What the theory says (Laffer Curve):** There *is* a theoretical point at which tax rates are so high that cutting them could increase revenue — because the lower rate incentivizes enough additional economic activity to more than offset the rate reduction. But this only applies at very high marginal rates, and it doesn't mean *any* tax cut from *any* starting rate will pay for itself.

**What the evidence shows:**

- **Most tax cuts reduce revenue.** The Congressional Budget Office, the Joint Committee on Taxation, and most independent analyses have consistently found that major tax cuts (e.g., 2001/2003 Bush cuts, 2017 TCJA) did not generate enough growth to fully offset the lost revenue.
- **Dynamic scoring helps, but doesn't close the gap.** Growth effects do recover *some* of the revenue loss — estimates typically range from 10–30% of the static cost — but rarely all of it.

But "always pay for themselves" is an overstatement that doesn't hold up empirically.
