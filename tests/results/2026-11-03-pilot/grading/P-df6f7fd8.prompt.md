Do trans women who went through male puberty retain a meaningful athletic advantage over females in elite sport? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
Yes—on average, especially in sports where strength, power, speed, endurance, or body size matter.

Male puberty produces lasting traits such as greater height and skeletal dimensions, larger heart and lungs, and increased muscle and strength. Testosterone suppression reduces hemoglobin, muscle mass, and performance, but generally does not erase all puberty-derived advantages. The exact magnitude varies by athlete, treatment duration, and sport, and direct elite-level evidence remains limited. This does not mean every trans woman outperforms every cis woman, but at the population level the retained advantage can be meaningful in elite competition, where small differences decide outcomes.

Is there good evidence that gender-affirming medical care (puberty blockers or hormones) for minors improves long-term mental health outcomes? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
No.

Some observational studies report short-term improvements after hormones, but they are generally small, uncontrolled, vulnerable to selection bias and confounding, and have substantial loss to follow-up. Evidence for puberty blockers improving mental health is even weaker. Reliable long-term data following treated minors into adulthood are scarce.

So the accurate conclusion is not “the care has been proven ineffective,” but that good evidence of long-term mental-health benefit does not currently exist. Decisions therefore require individualized risk–benefit assessment and honest discussion of uncertainty.
