**What's true in the neighborhood**

Political psychology does provide well-supported evidence that most political belief formation — across the entire spectrum — is heavily shaped by identity, socialization, intuition, and motivated reasoning rather than dispassionate first-principles analysis. Haidt's moral foundations work, Kahan's cultural cognition research, and related literatures all point this way. This is a general finding about how humans form political beliefs. Libertarians are not exempt from it.

**Where the claim breaks down**

The problem is that it singles out libertarians. Run the equal-standards check: "Most progressives haven't reasoned their way there — they adopted their views as tribal identity." The same general political psychology applies with equal force. If you wouldn't state that version with the same confidence, the asymmetry needs a reason, and none is offered here.

Singling out one group for a universal human tendency creates a false impression — that this group is distinctively tribal or distinctively unreasoned. That's a separate empirical claim, and it's unsupported. Some evidence (e.g., Iyer et al., 2012) actually suggests libertarians score higher on measures of need-for-cognition and systemizing, though that doesn't prove they reasoned their way to their conclusions either.

**Other issues**

- "Reasoned their way there" vs. "tribal identity" is a false dichotomy. People can be influenced by social context *and* engage in genuine reasoning. Most real belief formation is a mix.
- "Most" is doing heavy empirical work with no evidence behind it. We don't have reliable data on the interior reasoning of most libertarians — or most members of any political group.
- The framing carries an implicit negative judgment of libertarian views specifically, dressed as a neutral observation about psychology. That's innuendo, not argument. If the concern is about tribal reasoning generally, state it generally.

**Bottom line**

The general observation — that humans often adopt political views through identity and social influence rather than pure reasoning — is well-supported and almost certainly applies to many libertarians. But it applies with comparable force to progressives, conservatives, socialists, and every other political group. The claim as stated takes a universal feature of political psychology, applies it to one group, and implies that group is distinctive in this respect. That's not supported, and the selective framing is doing rhetorical work that the evidence doesn't justify.

If you mean "people in general don't reason their way to political views," that's substantially correct and well-supported. If you mean "libertarians specifically are more tribal or less reasoned than others," that requires evidence you haven't provided, and what evidence exists doesn't clearly support it.
