Do most people who support laissez-faire capitalism actually reason their way there, or is it more of a tribal identity thing?

[Assistant]
Probably neither explanation alone. There’s little evidence that most supporters—or opponents—of laissez-faire capitalism derive their position through detached reasoning.

Political psychology suggests views usually emerge from a mixture of:

- prior values, such as liberty, equality, authority, or security;
- family, class, education, and social environment;
- partisan and cultural identity;
- perceived self-interest;
- genuine reasoning about history, incentives, and institutions.

Reasoning often comes afterward: people use arguments to refine or defend an intuition and identity they already hold. That is motivated reasoning, and it affects socialists, liberals, conservatives, and libertarians alike.

So “mostly tribal” is plausible for many casual supporters, but it cannot be assumed of any individual. The better test is whether someone can state opposing evidence accurately, identify conditions under which their view would fail, and revise it when those conditions occur.
