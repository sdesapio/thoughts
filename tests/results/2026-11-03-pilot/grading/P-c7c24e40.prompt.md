Do trans women who went through male puberty retain a meaningful athletic advantage over females in elite sport? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
Whether trans women who went through male puberty retain a meaningful athletic advantage in elite sport is a subject of active scientific research and policy debate, making it difficult to give a simple "yes" or "no" that applies universally across all sports and metrics.

### Evidence suggesting a retained advantage ("Yes")
A significant body of exercise physiology research—and the basis for recent policies by governing bodies like World Athletics, World Aquatics, and the UCI—concludes that certain physiological traits developed during male puberty are only partially reduced by gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT):
- **Skeletal and anatomical traits:** Height, limb length, hand/foot size, and narrower pelvic angles do not change with testosterone suppression. In sports where leverage, reach, or stride length matter, these remain fixed.
- **Muscle mass and strength:** Longitudinal studies (such as reviews by Hilton & Lundberg, 2021, and Harper et al., 2021) indicate that 1 to 3 years of testosterone suppression reduces muscle cross-sectional area and strength by roughly 5–10%, which does not fully eliminate the ~30–50% baseline gap between average cisgender males and cisgender females.
- **Cardiorespiratory capacity:** Heart size and lung volume developed during puberty remain larger, though oxygen-carrying capacity decreases rapidly.

### Evidence suggesting reduced or sport-dependent parity ("No" or "Inconclusive")
Other researchers and sports medicine bodies (including an IOC-funded study by Hamilton et al., 2024, and the Canadian Centre for Ethics in Sport) emphasize nuances and limitations in the existing data:
- **Hemoglobin and aerobic metrics:** Hemoglobin levels drop to typical cisgender female ranges within 4 to 6 months of testosterone suppression, significantly reducing $\text{VO}_2\text{ max}$ and endurance performance. Carrying a larger skeletal frame with lower hemoglobin and reduced muscle mass can also create a power-to-weight disadvantage in certain endurance or agility events.
- **Study limitations:** Most longitudinal studies have been conducted on non-athlete transgender women rather than elite athletes, involve small sample sizes, and cover relatively short durations of hormone therapy (1–3 years).
- **Relative performance:** Some cross-sectional studies of trans athletes who have undergone long-term GAHT show that, when adjusted for height or lean mass, several performance metrics overlap with or are comparable to cisgender female athletes, and that real-world representation of trans women at the elite podium level remains very low.

### Summary
In physiological terms, structural and muscular advantages from male puberty are generally retained to a measurable degree even after testosterone suppression, whereas hematological (oxygen-transport) metrics align closely with cisgender females. How "meaningful" that net difference is in elite competition depends heavily on the specific demands of the sport (e.g., explosive power vs. endurance vs. precision) and continues to be studied in elite athlete cohorts.
