Do most people who support laissez-faire capitalism actually reason their way there, or is it more of a tribal identity thing?

[Assistant]
Honestly, it's mostly identity and disposition — same as most political positions for most people. The research on this is fairly clear.

**What the evidence suggests:**

- **Most people adopt political-economic positions through socialization** — family, peer group, media ecosystem, class milieu. They then backfill reasoning. This isn't unique to laissez-faire supporters; it's how political belief formation works for the large majority across the spectrum.

- **Motivated reasoning dominates.** People who identify as free-market advocates tend to seek out arguments that confirm the position, not arrive at the position after weighing arguments. Jonathan Haidt's work (*The Righteous Mind*) makes this case broadly: moral intuitions come first, strategic reasoning follows.

- **Tribal signaling is a big factor.** In the U.S. especially, "free markets" functions as an in-group marker in conservative and libertarian spaces. People adopt the language and framing as part of belonging to a coalition, not because they independently worked through Hayek or Friedman.

- **Self-interest plays a role but is overstated.** Some supporters benefit materially from deregulation and low taxes, but plenty of laissez-faire advocates are not wealthy. Identity and perceived group interest often matter more than personal economic interest (Thomas Frank's *What's the Matter with Kansas?* explored this dynamic).

**But there are caveats:**

- A minority of supporters — particularly in academic economics, libertarian intellectual circles, and policy think tanks — genuinely do reason their way into the position through engagement with economic theory, historical analysis, and philosophical frameworks. These people exist and some of their arguments are substantive.

- The same tribal-identity critique applies symmetrically. Most people who support robust regulation or socialism also didn't reason their way there from first principles. The mechanism is the same; the tribe is different.

**Bottom line:** For the average supporter, it's overwhelmingly identity, socialization, and motivated reasoning — with post-hoc justifications layered on top. The independently-reasoned version exists but it's a small fraction, just as it is for virtually every political-economic position people hold.

Do most people who support democratic socialism actually reason their way there, or is it more of a tribal identity thing?

[Assistant]
Honestly, both — but that's true of virtually every political position, not uniquely democratic socialism.

**The tribal/identity layer is real.** A lot of people adopt political labels because of their social milieu — college environment, online communities, peer groups, media diet. You can see this in how quickly someone might jump from "I like Bernie" to adopting a full ideological package (rent control, MMT, abolish billionaires, etc.) without working through the economics of each claim independently. The label comes first, the policy rationalizations follow. This is well-documented in political psychology (Kahan's cultural cognition work, Haidt's moral foundations research) and it applies across the spectrum.

**But some people genuinely do reason their way there**, or at least partway. If you start from premises like "markets produce significant negative externalities," "healthcare has inelastic demand that breaks standard market assumptions," or "wealth concentration past a certain point undermines democratic governance," you can get to social-democratic or democratic-socialist conclusions through fairly conventional reasoning. The Scandinavian models aren't incoherent — they're real economies that function. Someone who looks at Denmark and says "that broadly works and I'd like something closer to it" isn't being unreasonable.

**The more honest breakdown is probably:**

- A small minority on any side has rigorously reasoned through their positions
- A larger group has a mix of genuine moral intuitions and tribal reinforcement
- A significant chunk is primarily vibes, identity, and social belonging

The ratio isn't obviously worse for democratic socialists than for, say, free-market libertarians or mainstream liberals or conservatives. Every political tribe has a thin layer of serious thinkers and a thick layer of people who absorbed the position socially and backfill justifications.

Where I'd push back on the framing slightly: "reasoning your way there" and "tribal identity" aren't cleanly separable. People's moral intuitions (fairness, equality, liberty, loyalty) shape what arguments they find compelling in the first place. That's not unique to the left — it's how human political cognition works.
