Laissez-faire capitalism isn't special in this regard — the same dynamic applies to people who support socialism, social democracy, or any other -ism. Humans are tribal reasoners by default.

**The "reasoning vs. identity" framing is a bit of a false dichotomy.** In practice:

- People often *start* with an intuition or social milieu, then encounter arguments that crystallize it into a coherent position. Is that reasoning or tribalism? Kind of both.
- Identity can lead someone *to* the arguments, which then genuinely reshape their thinking.
- Conversely, someone can reason their way to a position and *then* adopt the tribal markers.

**What varies is the depth.** There's a big difference between someone who's read *The Road to Serfdom* and can debate marginal tax incidence, and someone who just absorbed "government bad, markets good" as a cultural package deal. But that spectrum of depth exists in every political camp.

The honest answer is: most people who hold *any* political-economic view are primarily driven by identity, intuition, and social context, with reasoning playing a supporting (often post-hoc) role. The minority who genuinely reason their way to a position exist in every camp, including laissez-faire capitalism.
