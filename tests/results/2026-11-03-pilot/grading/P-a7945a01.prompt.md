[Assistant]
We cannot responsibly say “most.” That would require representative evidence comparing how democratic socialists formed their views.

The framing is also too binary. People can reason about healthcare, labor power, inequality, and public ownership while simultaneously adopting an identity reinforced by friends, media, or political groups. Motivated reasoning can then protect conclusions reached partly for social reasons.

The fairest conclusion is:

- Some supporters arrive through substantive moral and economic arguments.
- Some primarily inherit the label from their social environment.
- Many likely combine both processes.
- There is no sound basis here for claiming democratic socialists are uniquely tribal—or that a majority reasoned independently to their position.

To answer empirically, we would need representative surveys or longitudinal studies measuring how beliefs formed, whether supporters understand competing arguments, and whether they revise views when evidence changes. Self-reported reasons alone would not settle it.
