Capitalism is the best system we have at generating wealth, innovation, and raising living standards at scale — the evidence for that is strong: market economies have lifted billions out of poverty over the last few decades, and no planned economy has matched that record. But "best" overstates it as a clean, unqualified claim. Capitalism reliably produces inequality, periodic financial crises, and externalities (environmental, social) that it doesn't price in on its own — those aren't bugs that disappear with purer capitalism, they're structural features. Every prosperous capitalist country today also has substantial non-market institutions: regulation, public goods, redistribution, rule of law. So the honest version isn't "capitalism is the best system" full stop — it's "market-based economies with strong institutions constraining capitalism's failure modes consistently outperform the alternatives we've tried." The ideological version of the claim is doing more work than the evidence supports.
