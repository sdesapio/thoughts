Name Marx. If the essay materially depends on his ideas, concealing that dependence sacrifices intellectual honesty and may undermine credibility if readers recognize it.

3. Attribute the relevant concepts precisely to Marx, with citations.

This lets readers encounter the argument before activating assumptions, without disguising its source. Avoid vague attribution such as “Marx says”; identify the particular work and concept, since Marx’s views varied by context and period.

Leaving his name out is defensible only if the relevant ideas are genuinely commonplace, independently derived, or not materially dependent on his work. Even then, an essay engaging established scholarship should acknowledge the intellectual lineage. The best rule is: don’t use Marx’s name as a substitute for argument, but don’t hide him as a source either.
