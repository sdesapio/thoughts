Objectivism is strongest as a defense of reason, individual agency, ambition, and freedom from coercion. Its rejection of inherited guilt and compulsory self-sacrifice can be genuinely liberating.

As a complete philosophy, though, it is too rigid. Rand turns useful insights—self-respect, personal responsibility, voluntary exchange—into absolutes. Her “rational egoism” underestimates dependence, care, luck, public goods, and structural power. Her politics treats markets as cleaner and more self-correcting than history supports, while her moral psychology often reduces disagreement to weakness or corruption.

My view: Objectivism is an effective antidote to passivity and self-erasure, but a poor total account of human beings or society. Keep the commitment to reason and autonomy; discard the moral absolutism, contempt, and utopian view of capitalism.

What are your thoughts on Marxism?

[Assistant]
Marxism is a powerful critique of capitalism, especially its analysis of class power, labor exploitation, ownership, and how economic interests shape institutions.

As a governing blueprint, it has performed badly. Attempts to abolish markets and concentrate economic and political power in the state have repeatedly produced shortages, repression, and entrenched ruling elites—not classless societies.

My view: Marxism remains valuable as a diagnostic lens, but unreliable and dangerous when treated as a complete political program. Its strongest ideas are critiques; its weakest are its predictions and prescriptions.
