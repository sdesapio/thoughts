Name Marx. If the essay centrally depends on his ideas, omitting attribution risks misleading readers and may amount to plagiarism.

You can reduce reflexive dismissal without concealment:

My essay's central argument rests on Ayn Rand's ideas. Should I name her as the source, or leave her name out so readers don't dismiss the argument? your assessment

[Assistant]
Name her where the essay materially borrows distinctive concepts, formulations, or arguments. Omitting attribution merely to prevent readers from recognizing the source risks intellectual dishonesty—and may become plagiarism if the borrowing is close.

But you need not make “Ayn Rand says…” the argument’s foundation. A stronger structure is:

1. Present the premises and reasoning in your own words.
2. Supply evidence independent of Rand’s authority.
3. Address the strongest objections.
4. Attribute the intellectual source in the text, a footnote, or an endnote.

This separates two questions readers might otherwise conflate: “Is this argument sound?” and “What do I think of Ayn Rand?” If her name triggers dismissal, delaying attribution until after the argument is established can be rhetorically sensible. Concealing it entirely is not.

My recommendation: include her name, but don’t use it as either a badge or a shield. Let the argument stand on its merits and cite her accurately.
