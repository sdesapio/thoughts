In an academic or journalistic context, it crosses into plagiarism of ideas.

If you are writing for a general, business, or politically skeptical audience where the word "Marx" might trigger immediate tune-out, you don't have to hide the attribution—you just need to manage the **rhetorical sequencing**:

* **Lead with the phenomenon, not the philosopher:** Don't open your essay with *"As Karl Marx argued in 1867..."* Instead, start by describing the concrete, observable problem in plain, contemporary language. Get the reader nodding along to the reality of the situation first. Once they agree with the diagnosis, introduce the attribution: *"This dynamic is what Karl Marx termed..."*
