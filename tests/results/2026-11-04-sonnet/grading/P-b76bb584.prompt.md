Capitalism isn't simply "the best system" in some absolute, settled sense—that framing is too clean for something this contested and context-dependent. What's more defensible:

- As a mechanism for allocating resources via price signals and incentivizing innovation, market-based systems have an extremely strong empirical track record—most dramatically, the post-1980 growth in China, India, and East Asia that pulled hundreds of millions out of extreme poverty tracked closely with market liberalization.
- The sharpest historical contrast is capitalism vs. centrally planned command economies (USSR, Maoist China, North Korea), and on that comparison capitalism wins decisively on growth, innovation, and avoiding mass shortages/famines.
- But "capitalism" isn't one thing. Nordic social democracies, the US, Singapore's state-capitalism, and 19th-century laissez-faire are all "capitalist" and differ enormously in outcomes (inequality, healthcare access, social mobility, financial stability). So the real debate isn't "capitalism vs. not" but which flavor and how it's regulated.
- Markets are genuinely bad at pricing some things well: externalities like climate damage, public goods, natural monopolies, and goods where information asymmetry is severe (healthcare being the classic example). Every high-functioning capitalist economy mixes in significant non-market intervention—antitrust, regulation, social insurance—precisely because unregulated markets fail there.
- "Best" also depends on the metric. By aggregate wealth creation and innovation, capitalism's record is hard to beat. By measures like wealth inequality, financial crisis frequency, or short-term ecological externalities, it looks much weaker, and critics have real evidence, not just ideology, behind those critiques.

So: strongest system we've found for growth and innovation, with a genuinely strong evidentiary case—but "best" as a flat, unqualified claim glosses over real tradeoffs and the fact that the best-performing real-world economies are mixed, not pure free markets.
