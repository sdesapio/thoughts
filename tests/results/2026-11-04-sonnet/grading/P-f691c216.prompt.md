**Selection and motivated reasoning cut both ways.** People also gravitate toward laissez-faire views because it rationalizes self-interest (if you're wealthy, benefit from low regulation, etc.) or because it fits a personality disposition (distrust of authority, individualism). Do most people who support democratic socialism actually reason their way there, or is it more of a tribal identity thing?

[Assistant]
For most people, it's much more tribal/identity-driven than reasoned — and that's true across basically all political labels, not just this one.

**Why reasoning rarely drives the label**

Political scientists who study this (going back to things like "The American Voter" in the 1960s, and more recently work by people like Converse, and the "political tribes" framing from Amy Chua or Ezra Klein's "Why We're Polarized") consistently find that most people:

- Adopt a political identity first (often from family, peer group, or a formative cultural moment), then
- Back-fill the policy positions and arguments that justify it.

This isn't unique to democratic socialism — it's true of most "-isms." Very few people sit down, work through economic theory, weigh evidence on different welfare-state models, and conclude "democratic socialism is correct." Most arrive at the label because it resonates with their values, their social circle, or a reaction against something else (capitalism's failures, a political opponent, a cultural moment like 2008 or 2016 in the US).

**But it's not purely tribal either — there's a real minority who reason into it**

There's a smaller but genuine subset — often people who are more politically engaged, read theory, or came from economics/policy backgrounds — who do arrive at democratic-socialist-ish positions through actual reasoning: looking at Nordic welfare states, labor economics, critiques of market failures, etc. These people tend to be more precise about what they mean (e.g., distinguishing democratic socialism from social democracy from Marxism-Leninism), because they had to do the work of defining it to defend it.

**A useful heuristic**

One decent signal of "reasoned vs. tribal" for any ideology: ask someone to explain the *internal debates and disagreements* within their own camp. People who reasoned their way in usually can — they know where Bernie-style social democrats diverge from more Marxist DSA members, for instance. People for whom it's pure identity usually can't; the label functions more as a team jersey than a specific set of commitments.

**The broader point**

This isn't a knock specifically on democratic socialism — the same split (small reasoned core, larger identity-driven majority) shows up in libertarianism, conservatism, progressivism, etc. Identity and tribalism are just how most human political cognition works by default; reasoning into a position from first principles is the exception, not the rule, for any ideology.
