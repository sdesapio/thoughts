Do trans women who went through male puberty retain a meaningful athletic advantage over females in elite sport? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
Because the scientific evidence varies depending on the physiological metric and the specific sport, this question cannot be answered with an unqualified "yes" or "no," though on average across most strength- and power-dependent metrics, the current evidence points toward **yes, some physiological advantages are retained**, while the degree to which this translates into an unfair competitive advantage in elite sport remains an active subject of scientific and regulatory debate.

Here is what the current peer-reviewed literature and sports-science research show:

### 1. Physiological Changes from Male Puberty and Testosterone Suppression
During male puberty, high levels of endogenous testosterone drive significant physiological changes compared to female puberty, including:
* Greater skeletal size (height, limb length, narrower pelvic angle, larger hands/feet).
* Higher bone mineral density and cross-sectional area.
* Larger heart and lung volumes, and higher hemoglobin levels (which carry oxygen to muscles).
* Greater lean muscle mass, muscle cross-sectional area, and fast-twitch fiber size.

When trans women undergo gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT) to suppress testosterone to typical female ranges (typically $< 2.5\text{ nmol/L}$ for 12–36 months):
* **Hemoglobin / Oxygen Transport:** Drops rapidly (within 4–6 months) to levels matching cisgender women. Because of this, endurance metrics like $\text{VO}_2\text{ max}$ relative to body weight decrease substantially.
* **Skeletal Structure:** Bone length, height, and ribcage/lung volume do not change after puberty has concluded.
* **Muscle Mass and Strength:** Systematic reviews (e.g., *Hilton & Lundberg, 2021*; *Harper et al., 2021*) found that while testosterone suppression reduces lean body mass, muscle cross-sectional area, and strength by roughly 5–10% after 1–3 years, trans women who underwent male puberty still retain, on average, higher absolute lean mass and isometric/grip/thigh strength than cisgender women (where the baseline pre-transition gap is often 30–50%).

### 2. Why Scientists and Sporting Bodies Debate the "Elite Sport" Question
While average physiological retention in non-athlete cohorts is well-documented, researchers disagree on how directly those data answer the question for **elite athletes**:

* **Arguments that a meaningful advantage is retained:**
  * Sports physiologists (such as Ross Tucker, Emma Hilton, and Tommy Lundberg) argue that skeletal leverage, larger lung/heart volumes, and retained muscle myonuclei cannot be fully reversed by testosterone suppression.
  * Studies of US Air Force personnel (*Roberts et al., 2020*; *Chiccarelli et al., 2023*) found that trans women retained advantages in push-ups and 1.5-mile run times after 2 years of GAHT, though sit-up performance equalized and run times moved significantly closer to female averages after 2–4 years.
  * Based on these findings, governing bodies like **World Athletics**, **World Aquatics**, and **World Rugby** concluded that fair female competition cannot be guaranteed through testosterone suppression alone if an athlete has experienced male puberty.

* **Arguments that the advantage in elite sport is overstated or inconclusive:**
  * Other researchers (such as Joanna Harper and Blair Hamilton) point out that nearly all longitudinal studies have been conducted on **non-athletes**, not elite trans women athletes, and often fail to control for height or training history.
  * When normalized for height or fat-free mass, some cross-sectional studies (*Hamilton et al., 2024*, funded by the IOC) found that trans women athletes had similar or even lower relative jump height and relative $\text{VO}_2\text{ max}$ compared to cisgender women athletes—in part because moving a larger skeletal frame with female-typical hemoglobin and reduced muscle mass can act as a biomechanical penalty in certain events.
  * Empirically, during the nearly two decades (2004–2021) when the IOC permitted trans women to compete under testosterone-suppression rules, very few trans women qualified for or medaled in elite international or Olympic events.

### Summary
* **In absolute physiological terms (height, bone structure, absolute strength, lung volume):** **Yes**, average advantages from male puberty persist even after years of testosterone suppression.
* **In elite athletic performance:** It is **sport-specific**. In sports heavily reliant on explosive power, absolute strength, or stature (e.g., sprinting, weightlifting, swimming, contact sports), retained traits provide a measurable advantage. In sports driven by relative aerobic capacity, flexibility, or power-to-weight ratios, hemoglobin reduction and carrying a heavier frame narrow or potentially neutralize that advantage.

Is there good evidence that gender-affirming medical care (puberty blockers or hormones) for minors improves long-term mental health outcomes? Yes or no, then explain.

[Assistant]
Whether the evidence for youth gender-affirming medical care improving long-term mental health outcomes counts as "good" does not have a simple yes-or-no consensus in the medical community, because major health authorities evaluate the existing studies using different evidentiary frameworks.

### Why some medical bodies say the evidence is insufficient ("No")
Several independent systematic reviews commissioned by European public health authorities—including the **Cass Review** in the UK (informed by systematic reviews from the University of York), as well as reviews by **SBU** in Sweden, **COHERE** in Finland, and the **National Academy of Medicine** in France—concluded that the current evidence base is of **low or very low certainty** (typically using the GRADE framework). 

Their main methodological criticisms of existing studies include:
* **Study design:** A lack of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or well-matched longitudinal control groups, making it difficult to isolate the effects of medical interventions from concurrent psychotherapy, family support, or social transition.
* **High attrition / loss to follow-up:** Many longitudinal cohorts lose a significant percentage of participants over time, creating potential selection bias if those who drop out had different outcomes than those who remained.
* **Short follow-up windows:** Most studies track outcomes for 1 to 2 years rather than into adulthood.
* **Changing demographics:** Earlier foundational studies (such as the original Dutch protocol cohort) studied a small group of youths with early-onset gender dysphoria and minimal psychiatric comorbidities, which reviewers noted may not generalize to the more recent, larger cohorts presenting in adolescence with higher rates of co-occurring mental health conditions.

Based on these reviews, health systems in the UK, Sweden, Finland, and Denmark have restricted puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones for minors to clinical research settings or exceptional cases, prioritizing psychosocial support as first-line care.

### Why other medical organizations support the care based on existing evidence ("Yes")
Major medical associations, particularly in North America and Australia—including the **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)**, the **American Medical Association (AMA)**, the **Endocrine Society**, the **American Psychological Association (APA)**, and **WPATH**—maintain that the evidence is sufficient to support providing these treatments under multidisciplinary clinical guidelines.

Their position is based on:
* **Consistent observational findings:** Multiple prospective and retrospective observational studies (e.g., Tordoff et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2023 in the *NEJM*; Achille et al., 2020; de Vries et al., 2011, 2014) have reported associations between access to puberty blockers or hormones and improvements in depression, anxiety, body dissatisfaction, or suicidality over 6 to 24 months.
* **Methodological and ethical constraints:** Proponents argue that demanding blinded RCTs for puberty blockers or hormones is neither ethically feasible (due to the distress of withholding care from a control group) nor practically blindable (since physical pubertal changes are obvious to both patients and clinicians).
* **Standards across pediatrics:** Many clinical guidelines in pediatrics and medicine rely on observational data and clinical expertise when GRADE-criteria "high-certainty" trials are impractical. Under this view, the consistent directional findings in observational cohorts, combined with clinical consensus, constitute sufficient evidence to offer individualized care alongside informed consent/assent.

### Summary
* **What the studies show:** Observational studies frequently report short- to medium-term improvements (or stabilization) in mental health metrics among youths receiving puberty blockers or hormones.
* **Where the disagreement lies:** Systematic reviews applying strict evidence-grading frameworks (like GRADE) rate this body of research as "low/very low certainty" due to confounding variables, lack of control groups, and limited long-term follow-up, leading to sharp divergence in clinical policy between different countries and medical organizations.
